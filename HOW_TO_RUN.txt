VS Code: File > Open Folder > this folder.  Then: Terminal > Run Task...  and pick a task.
(Same from the Terminal: bash setup_mac.sh / bash upload_car.sh / bash upload_camera.sh / bash run.sh)

ONE TIME
  1  Setup (one time)                    installs Python libraries, arduino-cli, Arduino + ESP32 support (internet, ~15 min)

ARDUINO
  2  Upload car code (Arduino)           Arduino on USB. Close the car app first.
     Check motors                        battery on, wheels in the air
     Check servo

CAMERA (stage 3)
  3  Upload camera code (ESP32-CAM)      ESP32-CAM on its ESP32-CAM-MB board, USB to the Mac, Arduino unplugged.
                                         It asks for your WiFi (2.4 GHz). Empty = the camera makes its own WiFi
                                         "FlyCar-CAM" (password change-me-123): then connect the Mac to that WiFi.
                                         At the end it prints the camera address: open it in Safari, you see video.
     Then take it off the MB board, power it from the LM2596, mount it on the servo.
     The Mac must be on the same WiFi as the camera.

DRIVE
  4  Start the car app                   buttons STAGE 1 / STAGE 2 / STAGE 3 / STOP / swap eyes
                                         keys: 1 2 3, space = STOP, s = swap eyes
     Start the app WITHOUT the robot     simulated car, to try everything

NO CABLE (WiFi through the ESP32-CAM)
     1  Upload the new camera code once (bash upload_camera.sh).
     2  Wire it: PINS.txt -> ESP32-CAM. Battery + -> Arduino VIN. USB cable out.
     3  config.py:  PORT = "wifi"      (it uses the camera address from CAMERA)
     4  Car on, Mac on the same WiFi as the camera, then bash run.sh. All 3 buttons work.
     WiFi drops -> the Arduino stops the motors by itself (0.3 s), the app reconnects.
     Stage 1 with no laptop at all: START_MODE = 1 at the top of car.ino, upload, power on.

RADAR (stages 1 and 2)
     The sensor sweeps 5 directions: far left 70, left 35, straight, right 35, far right 70 degrees.
     The window draws the last 2.5 s of echoes as fading dots, like a ship's radar.
     Leave the sensor's 4 wires LOOSE (a small loop), or they pull the sensor back when it turns.
     Wrong way / not straight: Check servo, then LEFT_SIGN / CENTER_DEG / NEAR_DEG / WIDE_DEG in car.ino.

SERVO STOPS TURNING WHILE DRIVING
     Run "Check power" (car on a box, wheels in the air). It shows the lowest 5 V while the motors run.
     Below 4.5 V = the battery can't feed motors + servo: charge it, servo + on the LM2596 5 V,
     a 470-1000 uF capacitor across servo + and -. The app also warns "5 V DROPPED" while driving.

BATTERY
     Measure the pack with the multimeter (red on +, black on -). Write the number in
     arduino/car/car.ino:  const float BATTERY_V = 12.0;   then upload the car code again.
     The code scales the motor power to it (motors get at most ~6 V, whatever the pack).
     More than 20 V: NOT on Arduino VIN (its limit). Ask first.

STATUS LINE (bottom of the window; also printed in the terminal and saved in the CSV "event" column)
     LINK LOST ...            WiFi to the car dropped. Motors already stopped. Reconnects by itself.
     Arduino restarted Nx     the battery voltage dipped (weak/empty pack, loose wire). Charge it.
     NO SONAR DATA            no distance readings for 1.5 s: sensor wires, or the Arduino is busy/stuck.
     CAMERA FROZEN            no new picture for 0.5 s (stage 3): the car stops until pictures come back.
     STUCK                    the stuck reflex is driving (backs up and turns), not the brain/rules.

SOUND ("tiiii" while driving)
     That is the motors singing at the Arduino's 980 Hz PWM. Harmless. To silence it: move 3 wires
     (L298N ENA D5 -> D11, ENB D6 -> D3, servo signal D3 -> D4), set QUIET_WIRING to 1 at the top
     of car.ino, upload. Or simply record the video with a voice-over / music instead of car sound.

Every run is saved in runs/ (one CSV per run).
Settings: config.py (camera address, camera upside down, speeds). Arduino: top of arduino/car/car.ino (pins, servo angles, stage 1 rules).
